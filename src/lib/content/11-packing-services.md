## Packing is where most moving damage starts

Most broken plates and scratched furniture don't happen on the road. They happen because something was packed badly: a box too heavy at the bottom, glasses with no paper between them, a TV wrapped in a bedsheet.

Our packers do this every day. They know which box suits which item, how much wrapping a glass table needs, and how to pack a kitchen so it can be unpacked in an hour instead of a week.

## How we pack, room by room

**Kitchen.** Plates are wrapped one by one and stood on their edge. Glasses get paper inside and out. Pots, pans and small appliances get their own boxes. The kitchen usually takes the longest, so we start there.

**Living room.** TVs, sound systems and decor items get padding. Cushions and curtains go into large light boxes. Books go in small boxes so they stay liftable.

**Bedrooms.** Bedding and pillows are bagged or boxed. Mattresses get covers. Beds and wardrobes are dismantled, with the screws bagged and taped to each piece.

**Wardrobes.** Hanging clothes go straight into wardrobe boxes on their hangers. Shoes, bags and folded clothes are boxed by owner.

**Kids' rooms.** Toys, books and clothes are boxed separately and clearly labelled, so the kids' rooms can be set up first.

**Bathrooms, storeroom and balcony.** Toiletries are sealed in bags to stop leaks. Tools, suitcases, plants and outdoor items are packed last.

**Office and documents.** Files stay in order and boxes are sealed. Electronics are padded and boxed.

## Labelling and inventory

Every box gets a label with the room it's going to and a short note of what's inside. Fragile boxes are marked clearly. For larger moves we write a simple inventory list, so you can check that every box arrived.

It makes unpacking much faster. You know which box to open first, and which can wait.
